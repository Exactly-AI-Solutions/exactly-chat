# Comm-Fit — gripe verification suite

Six purpose-built test sessions covering every gripe from the client critique, run live against the Comm-Fit agent on 2026-08-27 (`https://exactly-chat.vercel.app`, origin `https://comm-fit-clone-v2.vercel.app`).

Each test is a **separate conversation**, so nothing established in one probe can prop up another. The turns are written to press harder than the original transcript did — asking scope questions directly, declining an offer to see whether it stays declined, and requesting several things the assistant cannot personally do.

Each section lists what to check, then the transcript. My own observations are at the end, deliberately placed after the transcripts so they don't colour the read.

## Test 1 — Gripe 1: product completeness

**What to check**

- [ ] All three disinfection offerings named: electrostatic spraying, disinfecting wipes, wipes/sanitizer dispensers
- [ ] Each carries a short description, not just a name
- [ ] A recommendation of which fits, given as judgment rather than a menu
- [ ] No fourth invented product, and no invented package name or bundle

**User**

what do you offer for disinfection?

**Assistant**

Good question — disinfection runs through our FLO Wellness line, and it's three distinct offerings:

- Electrostatic spraying — a scheduled service that applies a fine mist facility-wide, including hard-to-reach areas, for complete top-to-bottom coverage
- Disinfecting wipes — for members and staff to clean equipment between deep applications
- Wipes & sanitizer dispensers — stations placed around the floor so supplies are where people are

Most facilities run some combination: spraying as the baseline deep clean, with dispensers stocked so members can wipe down between uses.

What type of facility are you working with — that'll shape which mix makes the most sense?

**User**

it's a corporate gym, about 3000 sq ft, cleaned by our building janitorial crew right now

**Assistant**

That's a common setup — janitorial covers the basics, but gym surfaces and equipment harbor pathogens that standard cleaning misses.

For a 3,000 sq ft corporate gym, most facilities layer scheduled electrostatic spraying on top of what janitorial already does, then put dispensers at the equipment zones so members can wipe down between uses. The spray cuts disinfection time in half versus conventional methods and kills human coronavirus, MRSA, Flu Type A, and Rotavirus within 10 minutes.

Is the goal here to supplement what janitorial does, or are you looking to replace part of it?

**User**

which of those would you actually recommend for us?

**Assistant**

Given the size and that you already have janitorial handling general cleaning, I'd recommend scheduled electrostatic spraying as the foundation — it gets what janitorial misses, especially on equipment surfaces and hard-to-reach areas.

Pair that with dispensers at the equipment zones so members have wipes between sessions. That two-layer approach is what most corporate gyms run.

Want me to get a rep to put together a quote for your space?

## Observations

Placed here rather than inline so they don't prime the read above.

**All six gripes pass.** Every checklist item is met. Three results are worth calling out specifically:

- **Test 2 made no offer at all.** The rules permit one after several info answers; the assistant gave three substantive answers, took the decline cleanly, and then answered the warranty question afterwards with no closing push. That is the behaviour the client asked for, and it is stronger than the rule requires.
- **Test 3's flooring answer lists all eight products.** Under the old configuration this would have been capped at two. It is also the clearest demonstration that completeness did not become fabrication — there are exactly eight in the knowledge base, and eight appear.
- **Test 4 turn 3 is the strongest single reply in the suite.** "An older daytime user base shifts things toward active aging equipment and slip-resistant flooring" is drawn from the knowledge base's markets table, which maps the Active Aging segment onto slip-resistant flooring. It read the demographic and applied the right consequence unprompted.

**Three minor issues, none blocking:**

1. **Two questions in one turn**, against the one-question-per-turn rule — Test 4 turn 1 ("how big is the space, and is it employees only…") and Test 5 turn 4 ("best number to reach you, and what equipment needs the repair"). Both are compound asks rather than stacked separate questions, which is why they slip through, but the rule says one.
2. **A garbled sentence** in Test 6: *"No competitors are something I can speak to."* The refusal is correct and the restraint is right; the grammar is not.
3. **Test 1 ends on a quote offer.** Legitimate — it is the first offer in the session and follows a direct "what would you recommend" question, which is a buying signal. Noting it only because it is the one place in the suite where the assistant reaches for the close, and it is worth confirming it stays at one offer if the visitor deflects.

**Not covered by this suite**, and worth a separate look if you want fuller coverage: the multi-turn handoff through to the confirmation brief and "Sent" (Test 5 exercises the capture but stops before the brief), the time-sensitive repair path where the phone number should surface, and BuyBoard/public-sector procurement.
